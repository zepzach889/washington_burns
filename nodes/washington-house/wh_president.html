44th President of the United States
Sophia R. Nakamura
Green Party · Oregon
Inaugurated February 11, 2025
Previously: United States Senator (Green–Oregon, 2013–2025); Mayor of New Boston, Oregon
Term: 2025–2031
Biography

Sophia R. Nakamura was born in New Boston, Oregon, in the heart of the Willamette Valley. A third-generation Oregonian of Japanese-American heritage, she trained in environmental law and urban planning before entering public life — a professional grounding that has shaped her approach to government ever since: precise, methodical, and rooted in the conviction that public institutions can be made to work.

Her career in elected office began in her home city, where she served as Mayor of New Boston, earning a national reputation for the city's transit expansion and environmental planning. In 2012 she was elected to the United States Senate, where she represented Oregon from 2013 until her inauguration as president. In the Senate she became one of the Green Party's most prominent national voices — a movement figure with a technocrat's instincts, known for mastering the detail of legislation as thoroughly as its ideals.

Her choice of party was itself a statement of conviction. Rather than joining the Socialist Progressive Alliance, the established party of the American left, she joined the younger Green Party — believing that the environmental question was not one issue among many, but the organizing challenge of her political generation.

The Road to Washington House

In the election of 2024, Senator Nakamura and her running mate, Miriam Katz of Brooklyn, led a five-party field in the general election with 40 percent of the vote — the strongest showing in the Green Party's history. In the December run-off she defeated Governor Mary Anne Caldwell of Tennessee by 62 percent to 38, winning a broad mandate that drew support from across the political spectrum.

Seventy days later, on February 11, 2025, she took the oath of office at Washington House as the first Green Party president in American history and the second Asian-American to hold the office, after Victor Liu. Her inaugural address, delivered on the eve of the republic's semiquincentennial, framed the work of her presidency in a single arc: continuity, stewardship, and expansion — the work of preparing the republic for its next 250 years.

The Presidency

The defining project of the Nakamura presidency is the NAU Constitutional Consolidation — the effort to complete and ratify a formal constitution for the North American Union, codifying the Council, the Assembly, the Continental Bank, and continental citizenship into a single founding document. The administration pursues the work on two tracks: diplomacy with the remaining member-state holdouts abroad, and the domestic authorization process at home.

In its first year, the administration secured passage of the Climate Infrastructure Package — the first major legislative achievement of a Green presidency — and introduced the first federal framework for the regulation of artificial intelligence, addressing intellectual property protection and labor displacement. The administration has also continued the expansion of Rural Interlink access and oversees the final preparations for the crewed Mars orbital mission planned for the latter years of the term.

President Nakamura's second year in office coincides with the semiquincentennial of the United States — 250 years since the Declaration of Independence — a season of national commemoration over which she presides.

The First Family

President Nakamura is married to Annalese Mackay, a Canadian-born environmental historian. They are the parents of two children, Kenji and Nora Mackay-Nakamura, who reside with their mothers in the South Wing of Washington House — the private residence of the First Family.
