Washington House is among the most visited landmarks in Franklin, welcoming the public into fourteen rooms of the State and Diplomatic Wings — the ceremonial heart of the American presidency. Tours are free of charge, and the gardens of President's Square are open every day of the year.

Public Tours

Guided tours of Washington House are offered Tuesday through Saturday, 9 a.m. to noon, and take visitors through the public rooms of the State Wing and the Diplomatic Wing. Tours are free of charge; advance booking through the Visitor Office is strongly recommended, as same-day places are limited. All visitors pass through security screening at the Visitor Pavilion on the east side of President's Square.

The Executive Wing — the working offices of the presidency — and the Private Wing, the residence of the First Family, are not open to the public.

Grand Reception Hall
The great ceremonial room of the State Wing — the site of state dinners, addresses, and the swearing-in of successor presidents since 1873.
State Dining Room
The formal dining room of the Diplomatic Wing, recently restored, where visiting heads of state and government are hosted.
The Ballroom
The largest room in the Diplomatic Wing, used for receptions, formal balls, and gatherings of the states' governors.
The Union Gallery
A rotating selection from the national art collection, curated with the Museum of the United States.
The Union Gallery

Housed in the Diplomatic Wing, the Union Gallery displays a rotating selection of works from the national art collection — landscapes, portraits of historical figures, and works commissioned for the building itself. The selection is overseen by the Office of Presidential Properties in consultation with the Museum of the United States, and changes several times a year, giving returning visitors something new on every tour.

The Grounds

The gardens of President's Square — the great plaza where Union Avenue meets the north face of Washington House — are open to the public daily during daylight hours, no booking required. At their center stands the square's famous fountain and statue, traditionally identified as George Washington, though the original commission documents are famously ambiguous on the subject.

The formal gardens behind the House may be visited by guided garden tour, arranged through the Visitor Office in the same manner as tours of the state rooms. Each First Family has traditionally made some addition or alteration to the gardens, making them a living record of the building's occupants — garden tours are a favorite of returning visitors for precisely this reason. The gardens also serve as the setting for official outdoor receptions, during which tours are suspended.

School & Group Visits

Washington House welcomes more than 40,000 student visitors each year. Educational programmes for school groups — including curriculum-linked tours on the presidency, the Constitution of 1861, and the history of the capital — are available by appointment through the Visitor Office. Group visits for organizations of ten or more may also be arranged by appointment, subject to the official schedule.

Accessibility

All fourteen public rooms on the tour route are step-free accessible, and wheelchairs are available at the Visitor Pavilion without charge. Assisted-listening devices and large-print tour guides are available on request. Visitors with particular access requirements are encouraged to notify the Visitor Office when booking so that arrangements can be made in advance.

Know Before You Go
All visitors pass through security screening at the Visitor Pavilion. Arrive at least twenty minutes before your tour time. Photography without flash is permitted in most public rooms; restrictions are posted where they apply. Large bags cannot be accommodated.
