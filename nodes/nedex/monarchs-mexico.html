The following is a list of the monarchs of the Empire of Mexico from its founding in 1835 to the present. All rulers are members of the House of Guerra, founded by General Raul Guerra upon his proclamation as Emperor in 1835. Following the Constitution of 1922, which established parliamentary supremacy and stripped the throne of meaningful executive powers, subsequent monarchs have reigned as constitutional heads of state. ✦ denotes a monarch who died in office.

Biographical Notes

Raul I (r. c.1835–c.1857)

General Raul Guerra founded the Empire of Mexico as the victor of the Mexican Civil War of 1835, defeating the American-backed Constitutionalist government of President Manuel Ortega despite significant US military intervention on the opposing side. Proclaimed Emperor Raul I, he established the House of Guerra as Mexico's imperial dynasty — an unusual founding in that the monarchy was born in direct military triumph over a government the United States had armed and fought alongside. The Second Mexican War (1845–1847) ended with the loss of Texas and California to independence; in its aftermath, Raul I's own military council compelled him to accept the Constitution of 1848 establishing a unicameral Cortes. He died approximately 1857 and was succeeded by his son.

Raul II (r. c.1857–c.1879)

Raul II pursued an aggressive policy of reasserting imperial authority against the growing reform movement in the Cortes. The constitutional crisis of 1868–1869, in which the Cortes nominated a prime ministerial candidate Raul II refused to confirm, left Mexico without a functioning government for several months before the Cortes relented and accepted the Emperor's counter-nominee, Marcos Vega. The episode became a landmark grievance for the reform movement even as it demonstrated the throne's formal supremacy.

Raul III (r. c.1879–c.1897)

Raul III proved a weaker figure than his predecessor. Without surviving direct heirs and facing an increasingly organised reform bloc in the Cortes, he made a series of grudging concessions over the course of his reign, including the inadvertently consequential appointment of reform-aligned Prime Minister Diego Herrera in 1882. He died without a direct heir in approximately 1897, having been forced to pass over his designated successor following a personal scandal; the throne passed to his nephew Jorge.

Jorge I (r. c.1897–1913)

Jorge I came to the throne as an unexpected heir with no traditional grooming for power, and his reign was defined by a sustained and deliberate program of constitutional reform. His dismissal of Prime Minister General Victoriano Cazares upon accession — a swift and pointed act that signalled the character of the reign to come — was followed by years of expanding the franchise, enacting labour protections, establishing the separation of church and state, and extending legal rights for women. His appointment of Federico Reyes as the first Partido Liberal Mexicano Prime Minister in 1910, and the enactment of universal male suffrage in 1912, represented the culmination of his reform program. He died unexpectedly of a short illness in 1913 at approximately 48 years of age.

Gabriella I (r. 1913–1959)

Empress Gabriella succeeded her father at the age of 26 and went on to define the modern Mexican state. Within months of her accession her younger brother declared himself Emperor Raul IV, claiming a woman could not legitimately hold the throne and launching eight years of civil war. The Mexican military — whose senior leadership Jorge I had quietly reshaped — largely backed Gabriella; the Texas Federation and the Union of the Californias declined to recognise Raul IV's claim and extended informal financial support to her government. Raul IV died of battlefield wounds in 1920; the Raulist cause collapsed definitively in 1921.

The Constitution of 1922, adopted in the civil war's aftermath, fundamentally transformed the Mexican state: parliamentary supremacy was formally established, stripping the throne of meaningful veto and appointment powers; the separation of church and state was codified; and universal suffrage — extended to women as well as men — was enshrined in law, making Mexico one of the earliest nations to adopt universal suffrage without restriction. Gabriella reigned for 46 years in total under this new constitutional order, the longest reign in the dynasty's history, and died in 1959 at the age of 72.

Jorge II (r. 1959–1988)

Son of Empress Gabriella; acceded to the throne in 1959 at the age of 49. Further details to be established.

Jorge III (r. 1988–2004)

Son of Jorge II; acceded to the throne in 1988 at the age of 56. Further details to be established.

Gabriella II (r. 2004–present)

Daughter of Jorge III; acceded to the throne in 2004 at the age of 32 and is the current incumbent monarch of the Empire of Mexico, now in her mid-fifties. Further details to be established.
