Union Avenue
Franklin, District of Washington

Union Avenue is the principal ceremonial thoroughfare of Franklin, District of Washington, running north to south from Congress Hall Square on the legislative bank of the Ohio River, across the George Washington Bridge, to Presidents' Square and Washington House on the executive bank.

Laid out as the spine of the original capital plan, it connects the seats of the legislative and executive branches along a single axis, and it carries the inaugural parade.

The original plan

The avenue was the organising decision of the capital's design. With Congress Hall sited on the north bank of the Ohio and the presidential mansion and executive departments on the south, a grand central avenue running north to south was to connect the two branches directly, and everything else in the plan was arranged around it.

The planners acknowledged that no engineer had yet succeeded in bridging the Ohio, and expressed confidence that the problem would be solved in time. It was solved in 1873, fifty-six years after the axis was drawn. For the intervening half-century the capital's principal thoroughfare was interrupted at its centre by a river, and the two halves of the government were joined by ferry.

The northern reach

The avenue begins at Congress Hall Square, where the building's grand staircase descends from the central colonnade toward the roadway. It runs south several blocks to the crossing of the Federal Green, the east–west parkland laid out in the early twentieth century as a deliberate counteraxis to the city's north–south spine.

At that intersection the avenue is flanked by two monuments. The Brandt Memorial, dedicated in 1910, stands on the west side at the Green's western anchor. The Global War Memorial stands opposite on the east. Before the latter's construction in the 1960s the Green lay open on that side, running uninterrupted for several blocks east to the Supreme Court Building — a vista that older residents of the capital still occasionally complain of having lost.

Constitution Avenue and the business district

South of the Green the avenue crosses Constitution Avenue, the capital's principal east–west road and the route of National Road One through the District. The intersection is the busiest in Franklin and the point at which the avenue's character changes.

Below Constitution the roadway passes through the city's central business district, the one stretch of its length that is neither ceremonial nor federal. Halfway between Constitution Avenue and the river it is flanked by the headquarters of the United States Post & Telephone Service on the west side and the Bank of the United States on the east, the two buildings facing one another across the avenue in a pairing that was not planned and is now among the most photographed views in the capital.

The crossing

The avenue crosses the Ohio by the George Washington Bridge, begun in 1866 and opened in 1873. The deck is aligned on the avenue's axis, so that the approach from either bank frames the terminus opposite.

The southern reach

On the south bank the avenue cuts through the Bureau District, where more than twenty federal departments and agencies maintain their headquarters, before terminating at Presidents' Square before Washington House.

Ceremonial use

The avenue's best-known function is the inaugural parade. The oath of office is administered at Congress Hall on the legislative bank; the new president then leads the procession south down Union Avenue, across the George Washington Bridge, to Presidents' Square, and ascends to the north balcony of Washington House to deliver the inaugural address.

The sequence is older than the bridge that now carries it. Before 1873 the procession crossed by ferry, an arrangement that was dignified in fine weather and memorably undignified otherwise, and which more than one nineteenth-century president is recorded as having endured rather than enjoyed.
