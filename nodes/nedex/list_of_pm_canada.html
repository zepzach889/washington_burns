Prime Ministers of Canada

Heads of government of the Dominion of Canada, 1865–present
Contents
  1. Role
  2. List of Prime Ministers
  3. Milestones
  4. See also

The Prime Minister of Canada is the head of government of the Dominion of Canada, chosen by the elected lower house of Parliament. The office has been held continuously since the first federal elections in 1865.

Role

The Prime Minister is chosen by Parliament's lower house and serves at its confidence. Parliamentary terms have a maximum length of five years, within which the Prime Minister may call an election at any time. There are no limits on the number of terms a Prime Minister may serve. Governments have fallen through confidence votes on multiple occasions throughout Canadian history, resulting in elections called before the five-year maximum.

List of Prime Ministers

#NameTermPartyNotes
1Joseph Criswell1865–1874FederationCo-founder of the Federation Party alongside Anthony Martin. Canada's first Prime Minister. Won the first federal election in 1869. Stepped aside in 1874.
2Anthony Martin1874–1879FederationRailroad entrepreneur and co-founder of the Federation Party. Served as the first Interior Minister under Criswell before becoming Prime Minister. Resigned due to failing health before the 1879 election. Died 1882.
3James Crawley1879–1883FederationWon the 1879 election narrowly as the Dominion Party made its first serious national showing. Hosted President Littlefield of the United States on his historic visit to Canada in April 1880, and announced the gift of the Freedom Triumphant statue on behalf of the Canadian and British governments. Lost the 1883 election.
4Sir William Brock1883–1888DominionFirst Dominion Prime Minister. Ontario-born merchant and banker. Pro-Crown, protective tariff advocate. Lost the 1888 election.
5Gregory Kent1888–1897FederationWon the 1888 election, reelected 1893. Present at the dedication of the Freedom Triumphant statue in New York City on July 4, 1890, representing Canada alongside Crown Princess Augusta Charlotte. Lost the 1897 election.
6Robert Dunmore1897–1904DominionNova Scotia-born, former military officer. Won 1897, won 1902. The Great European War (1899–1904) fell entirely on his watch. Lost a confidence vote in 1904.
7Thomas Reid1904–1909LabourFirst Labour Prime Minister. Born in Tecumseh, trade union lawyer. Minority government with Federation support. Lost the 1909 election when the Federation Party withdrew support and ran independently.
8Hugh Gallagher1909–1918FederationOntario-born lawyer. Won 1909 majority, reelected 1914. Present at the centennial celebrations marking one hundred years since the burning of Washington City. Called an early election in 1918 and lost to Labour.
9Arthur Baines1918–1925LabourBorn in Hamilton, Ontario, former schoolteacher turned trade union organizer. Won 1918 majority, reelected 1923. Built the foundations of Canadian labour law. Lost a confidence vote in 1925 when rural independent members defected over agricultural policy.
10Frederick Hollis1925–1932DominionNew Brunswick-born, business and banking background. Won 1925, reelected 1930 on an explicit platform of non-involvement in foreign conflicts. When the Global War began in 1932 and a British passenger vessel was sunk by Prussian U-boats, his position collapsed. Lost a confidence vote mid-1932.
11George Alderton1932–1941Federation / National UnityOntario-born, former Exterior Minister. Formed a National Unity Government immediately upon taking office, with cabinet seats drawn from all three major parties. His Interior Minister was James Carlisle, whose daughter Lady Elizabeth Carlisle had married Prince Charles of Britain in 1928. Won an uncontested continuation vote in 1937. Called an election within days of the formal peace treaty signing in late 1941, and lost to a Labour landslide.
12Margaret Dunbar1941–1952LabourFirst female Prime Minister. Born in Hamilton, Ontario, former labour lawyer. Swept the 1941 election. Won 1946 comfortably, won 1950 narrowly. Presided over postwar reconstruction, the Geneva settlement, and the early North American Union framework. Resigned 1952 due to failing health. Died 1958.
13Robert Parrish1952–1955LabourNova Scotia-born, former Finance Minister under Dunbar. Won the 1952 snap election on a continuity platform. Lost a confidence vote in 1955.
14Henri Lacombe1955–1964FederationFirst Francophone Prime Minister. Quebec City lawyer, bilingual and politically deft. Won 1960 with a solid majority. North American Union integration accelerated significantly on his watch. Retired voluntarily in 1964.
15Thomas Adeyemi1964–1974LabourFirst Black Canadian Prime Minister. Nigerian-Canadian, born in Ontario, former professor of economics. Won 1964 majority, reelected 1969. Retired 1974.
16Katherine Mercer1974–1983DominionBritish Columbia-born, fiscal conservative. Won 1974 majority, reelected 1979. Survived a confidence vote in 1981. Called an election in 1983 under mounting pressure, and lost to Labour.
17William Osei1983–2000LabourGhanaian-Canadian, born in Alexandria Province, former trade union lawyer. Won 1983 majority, reelected 1988, 1992, and 1997. At seventeen years, the longest-serving Prime Minister in Canadian history. Oversaw the full consolidation of the North American Union framework. Called an election in 2000 under party pressure and lost narrowly.
18Andrew Fitzroy2000–2010FederationOntario-born, former North American Union negotiator. Won 2000, reelected 2005. Stepped down as party leader in 2009 ahead of the 2010 election.
19Claire Beaumont2010–2020FederationSecond female Prime Minister. Quebec-born, former constitutional lawyer. Won 2010, reelected 2015. The first Prime Minister to formally acknowledge the Wikwemikong situation in the District of Manitoulin as an unresolved injustice requiring resolution. Retired 2020.
20James Corrigan2020–2025DominionAthabasca-born, fiscal conservative and mild North American Union skeptic. Minority government. Lost the 2025 election.
21Priya Anand2025–presentLabourFirst South Asian-Canadian Prime Minister. Born in Ontario to Indian immigrant parents. Won a majority in 2025. Current Prime Minister.

Milestones

  • First Dominion Prime Minister: Sir William Brock (1883)
  • First Labour Prime Minister: Thomas Reid (1904)
  • First female Prime Minister: Margaret Dunbar (1941)
  • First Francophone Prime Minister: Henri Lacombe (1955)
  • First Black Canadian Prime Minister: Thomas Adeyemi (1964)
  • Second female Prime Minister: Claire Beaumont (2010)
  • First South Asian-Canadian Prime Minister: Priya Anand (2025)
