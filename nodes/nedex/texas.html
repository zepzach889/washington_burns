Texas Federation
Federación de Texas · Federal presidential republic · Founded 1844 · North America
Contents
  1. History
  2. Government and Politics
  3. Geography

The Texas Federation (Federación de Texas) is an independent federal republic located in the south-central portion of the North American continent, bordered by the United States to the north and east, the Union of the Californias to the west, and the Empire of Mexico to the south. Its capital is Texopolis, situated on the Brazos River, and its largest city and principal port is Galvezport on the Gulf of Mexico. The Federation was established on January 3rd, 1844, when the Texas Free State and the Province of Texas jointly declared independence from the collapsing Mexican Republic in the face of Imperial Mexican invasion.

The country is a federal presidential republic, with executive power vested in a President elected to a six-year term with a two-term limit. The legislature, the National Assembly of Texas, consists of a lower House of Delegates apportioned by population and an upper Senate composed of three senators from each of the four member states: Austin, Brazos, Colorado, and Nuevo Mexico.

The Texas Federation is a founding member of the North American Conference and a close partner of both the United States and the Union of the Californias, with whom it shares the transcontinental railroad completed in 1881. Its economy is anchored by Gulf trade, agriculture, and the extractive industries of its western states.

History

The Texas Federation emerged from the chaos of the Second Mexican War (1844–1847), in which Texian forces allied with the United States to defeat the Empire of Mexico and secure independence. Its first president, Esteban Herrera, oversaw the drafting of the federal constitution and the difficult early compromises — most notably on the question of slavery, which remained legal in Austin state until abolished through compensated manumission in 1863 under President Stephen Austin. The completion of the Galvezport-to-San Francisco railroad in 1881 transformed the Federation's economy and its place in the North American order. The early twentieth century was marked by the Voss Affair, the largest political scandal in Texian history, which grew out of the exposure of the Matanzas Clique during the Caribbean War of 1905–1906. The Texas Federation participated in the Global War (1932–1940) as a member of the Allied coalition under President Marcos Delgado.

Government and Politics

The Texas Federation operates under its constitution, adopted September 8th, 1847, which establishes a separation of powers between the federal executive, legislature, and judiciary, with significant powers reserved to the four member states. The two dominant political parties are the Democratic-Nationalist Party and the Reform Party, which have alternated in government since the Federation's early years, with the DNP historically dominant.

Geography

The Federation spans a wide range of terrain, from the humid Gulf coastal plains of Austin and Brazos states in the east to the high desert and mountain ranges of Nuevo Mexico and Colorado states in the west. The Rio Grande forms the southern border with the Empire of Mexico. Major cities include Texopolis, Galvezport, San Antonio, Santa Fe, and New Frankfurt.
