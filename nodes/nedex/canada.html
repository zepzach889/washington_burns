Dominion of Canada

The Dominion of Canada is a self-governing dominion of the British Empire, occupying the northern portion of the North American continent. Bordered to the south by the United States of America, Canada extends from the Atlantic Ocean in the east to the Pacific Ocean in the west, and northward to the Arctic. Canada is a founding member of the North American Conference and a full member of the North American Union.

History

Canada was established as a unified dominion by the Articles of Dominion in 1864, bringing together the colonies of British North America under a federal system of government. From its founding, the country was shaped by the construction of the Trans-Canadian Railway, the settlement of the prairie interior, and the ongoing negotiation of its relationship with both the British Empire and the United States.

The twentieth century brought Canada through the Global War as a member of the Allied coalition, and into the postwar era as an increasingly influential partner in the North American framework. For a full account of Canadian history, see History of Canada.

Government

Canada is a constitutional monarchy and parliamentary democracy. The monarch of the United Kingdom serves as head of state, represented in Canada by the Governor-General, who is appointed by the Crown for a fixed term of seven years, offset from the parliamentary cycle.

The Parliament of Canada consists of an elected lower house, whose members choose the Prime Minister. Parliamentary terms have a maximum length of five years, within which the Prime Minister may call an election at any time. There are no limits on the number of terms a Prime Minister may serve.

Political Parties

Three major parties dominate Canadian parliamentary life. The Federation Party is a moderate nationalist centre party and the founding party of Confederation. The Labour Party represents the working class and progressive political tradition. The Dominion Party, historically the pro-Crown conservative party and informally known as the Tories, has in the modern era evolved into a straightforward fiscal conservative party.

A small Republican Party, advocating full Canadian independence from the monarchy, has existed as a persistent minor presence throughout Canadian political history.

Geography

Canada's borders with the United States do not follow a neat line. The border follows watersheds and natural features, meaning that portions of the northern prairie region lie within Canada that would in other circumstances form part of the United States, while small slivers above the 49th parallel in the west are within the United States.

The Great Lakes are almost entirely within Canadian territory — only Lakes Ontario and Erie share shorelines with the United States. The Province of Columbia, on the Pacific coast, extends south to the Columbia River, incorporating territory that includes the city of Pugetsburgh, the largest city on Canada's Pacific coast.

Capital

The capital of Canada is Aurora, located on Manitoulin Island in Lake Huron, within the District of Manitoulin — a federal district separate from all provinces. Aurora was purpose-built as a compromise capital following prolonged debate between Toronto and Montreal interests, with construction beginning in the mid-1880s and the city becoming operational as the seat of government in the early 1900s. Toronto served as the de facto capital during the construction period.

Provinces and Territories

Canada consists of fifteen provinces, one territory, and the federal District of Manitoulin. For a full list, see Provinces and Territories of Canada.
