Overview

The President of Ireland (Uachtarán na hÉireann) is the head of state of the Republic of Ireland. The office was established by the constitution of 1904 as a directly elected position with a seven-year term and a single-term limit. The President performs largely ceremonial functions but holds a limited set of reserved constitutional powers. Executive authority rests primarily with the Taoiseach as head of government.

Presidential elections are partisan — the major political parties field candidates and campaign for the office. Once elected, the President is expected to exercise the functions of the office above day-to-day partisan politics, though party affiliation is retained.

The founding decades of the office were dominated by two parties, Republican and Progressive. The political landscape widened during Seamus Cahill's presidency, when Ireland's 1934 entry into the Global War alongside the United States was managed through a national unity government drawing on four parties rather than the traditional two.
