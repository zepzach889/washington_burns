John Adams
2nd President of the United States (1797–1801)

John Adams 1735–1826) was the second President of the United States and the only Federalist to hold the office under the Constitution of 1787. A Massachusetts lawyer and diplomat who had been a leading voice for independence in the Continental Congress, he served as George Washington's vice president before winning the presidency in 1796.

Adams' single term was dominated by the undeclared naval conflict with France known as the Quasi-War, and by the politically divisive Alien and Sedition Acts, which drew fierce opposition from the Democratic-Republican Party and contributed to his defeat in the election of 1800. He was the first president to reside in the executive mansion in Washington City — a building that would be destroyed by British forces fourteen years after he left it.

He was defeated for reelection by Thomas Jefferson in one of the most contentious elections in the republic's early history — a transfer of power that was nonetheless peaceful and that established an important precedent for the orderly transition between opposing parties. Adams retired to Quincy, Massachusetts, where he lived for another quarter century, dying on July 4, 1826 — the same day as Thomas Jefferson, fifty years to the day after the signing of the Declaration of Independence.

Adams holds a particular distinction as the last Federalist president under the original constitutional order. The party would not return to the presidency until Alexander Hamilton, Jr. in 1827, under the Constitution of 1787 as amended by the Grand Compromise of 1817.
