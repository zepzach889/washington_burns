Overview

The Premier of the Union of the Californias is the head of government of the Union of the Californias, elected by majority vote of the Asamblea Nacional under a system of proportional representation. The office was established by the Constitution of 1847. The Premier serves at the confidence of the Asamblea Nacional and may be removed by a vote of no confidence at any time; if a government falls between scheduled elections, the Asamblea Nacional may assemble a new majority government from its existing membership, or vote to dissolve and hold a snap election.

Elections to the Asamblea Nacional are held every five years. Three parties have dominated the office since the republic's founding — the Centralist Party and the Federalist Party through the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, joined by the Labor Party as a governing force from 1932 onward. The following is a complete list of Premiers from the founding of the republic to the present day.

In recent years the office has taken on a growing continental dimension. California was among the earliest voices — alongside Canada and the Texas Federation — pushing for deeper NAU institutional integration, and the sitting Premier has continued to engage actively with the ongoing NAU Constitutional Consolidation discussions moving through the Assembly in Calumet.

Notes on the office

The numbering "7b" for Eduardo Reyes reflects his status as a caretaker premier who did not come to office through a general election. He is sometimes excluded from official counts, making Alcott either the eighth or ninth premier depending on the convention used. The Asamblea Nacional's official records list Reyes in the sequence.

Historical note
Two Premiers have died in office: Francisco Ruiz, killed in the San Francisco earthquake of April 18, 1906, and the 19th Premier, who died in 1979 shortly after becoming the first officeholder to win a third term.
