Sacred Canon Rules

These points are locked. They cannot be contradicted, revised, or worked around without explicit author decision.

Political Spectrum

SPA → Federalist → Democrat → JCA (left to right). SPA endorses Federalist in runoffs; JCA endorses Democrat. Never reverse.

Georgina Lincoln

Wins presidency 1958, inaugurated January 1959. First woman president. First Socialist president. Sacrosanct.

Jesse Pike

Outed as gay post-Global War. Becomes civil rights symbol. Elected Congress, Senate, presidency (1983–1989). Presides over marriage equality.

Henry Freeman

First elected Black president (1941–1947, Federalist). Peter Landon (1903–1905) was first by succession. Freeman's election is monumental but not shocking.

No Hard Jim Crow

Enforced segregation dead by early 1910s. Black Americans voting and participating fully since 1861. Do not import OTL racial history.

USRC

United States Railway Commission — one of America's oldest and most beloved institutions. Never dismantled. Only reformed at margins.

Single 6-Year Terms

US presidents serve one six-year term. No re-election. No second terms. Ever.

North American Union

Evolves from NAC framework into EU-like organization. Common currency, open borders, coordinated militaries, elected Assembly.

Political System

The Four Parties

Social Progressive Alliance (SPA)

Founded as alliance of socialist and progressive groups. Labor first, capital second. Nationalization, social welfare. Very socially progressive. Base: industrial workers, urban immigrants, farm laborers. Equivalent: democratic socialist.

Federalists

Oldest party. Strong central government, social reform, abolitionism, racial/gender equality. Anti-exploitation but pro-capitalism with mild regulation. Base: New England, Freedmen States, educated professionals, Black Americans.

Democrats

Pro-big government in infrastructure terms. Believe federal investment built the West. Pragmatic on social change. Base: West, infrastructure boosters, moderate farmers, urban machines. Natural governing party. Becomes center-right as JCA withers.

Jeffersonian Constitutional Alliance (JCA)

Pro-small government, states over federal, agrarian. Wants social reform slowed. Base: small farmers, rural whites, Western settlers. Withers after Nolan/Priest era. Never again a presidential contender after ~1970s.

Long-Term Party Evolution

As the JCA withers, space opens for a genuine Green Party which becomes a significant force by the 1990s–2000s, frequently in coalition with the SPA. By the 21st century the real competition is SPA vs. Federalist vs. Democrat in a three-way race.

Soft Canon

The Five-Pole World Order

The postwar world is genuinely multipolar — not a clean bipolar split. The fault line is more geographical great-power rivalry than ideological, with a subtle autocracy-vs-democracy flavor at the margins. The GPC is the primary arena where the world-order fight plays out.

The Liberal Spectrum

Running from most to least liberal/open:

NAU — Most Liberal

Democratic, deeply integrated, elected Assembly, common currency, open borders. The most institutionally advanced bloc.

UER — Very Liberal

Genuine elections, civil liberties, religious freedom restored post-Holtz. Communalist structure intact but reformed. Stable and confident postwar victor.

Britain — Liberal

Constitutional monarchy, parliamentary democracy. Managing imperial decline. More conservative establishment than NAU or UER.

Russia — Partially Liberal

Constitutional monarchy since 1854. Functioning Imperial Duma. Better than China, not as open as Britain. Independent swing actor.

China — Least Liberal (initially)

Constitutional monarchy but not free. Press control, restricted personal freedoms. Gradually liberalizes post-Choson War. Genuine democratization by 2019 Chinese Spring.

Key Rule

Sun bombs function as deterrent against major power hot conflicts throughout the postwar era. The Choson War (1966–67) is the primary test — the deterrent holds. Neither side uses them despite Beijing and Shanghai being bombed conventionally.

Members & Structure

Full Members

USA, Canada, Texas, California, Mexico (founding); Hawaii (1951). Each retains distinct identity, government, and considerable domestic autonomy.

Council

Small, appointed representatives from member states. Retains executive functions. Original governing body from 1945/46 charter.

Assembly

Directly elected. Budgetary oversight, treaty ratification. Established early 1970s under Langston. Fifty years old by Nakamura's presidency.

CDC

Continental Defense Command. Military arm. Federal-level commander. Made Choson War bombing decision collectively — all member governments stood behind it.

NAU Constitution Project (Nakamura Era)

The NAU Constitution consolidates sixty-plus years of accumulated treaties, amendments, and charter revisions into a single coherent founding document.

  • Codifies existing institutions — Council, Assembly, CDC, Continental Bank in single document with clear relationships
  • Formal NAU citizenship — alongside member state citizenship. Most symbolically significant new element.
  • Clarifies Assembly powers — expanding them somewhat. The "go further" side's win.
  • NAU judiciary/supreme court — most structurally significant new institution. Gives framework real enforcement mechanism.
  • Defines expansion framework — how new members join, what associate membership means going forward.
Integration Level by 2025

More integrated than OTL EU at its peak, less integrated than a true federal state. Member states retain distinct identities and governments. Something like: EU-plus.

Soft Canon

The Choson War (1966–1967)

The defining postwar conflict. Tests the NAU alliance, the GPC framework, and the sun bomb deterrent simultaneously.

Causes

  • Chinese hardliner faction ascendant after generational transition. Views NAU presence in Japan and Choson as permanent affront to Chinese regional hegemony.
  • 1940 ceasefire left Choson explicitly neutral but with no enforcement mechanism — twenty-plus years of Chinese economic penetration and political pressure follow.
  • Chinese-backed coup installs pro-Beijing government in Choson.

The War

  • NAU and Japan respond militarily — NAU honors commitment, Japan fights on its own doorstep.
  • CDC makes bombing decision collectively — Beijing and Shanghai bombed by conventional forces. All member governments stand behind decision.
  • Schultz presidency bears some political cost but not all — shared CDC responsibility is the mechanism.
  • Sun bomb deterrent holds — neither side uses them. Moderates' decisive internal argument: NAU retaliation would end the dynasty.

Resolution — Treaty of Delhi

  • India brokers the settlement — first major act of independent Indian diplomacy. Founding moment of Indian non-alignment doctrine.
  • China negotiates from genuine weakness for first time since 1932.
  • GPC-mandated settlement with real enforcement mechanisms — more durable than 1940 ceasefire.
  • Japan and Choson end up closer to NAU or more genuinely independent.
  • China keeps British treaty ports — no clean mechanism for return.

Consequences

GPC Strengthened

Emerges with genuine enforcement credibility for first time. Settlement runs through GPC with Russia and UER both backing it.

NAU More United

Alliance tested and holding. CDC acting decisively becomes proof of concept. Democratic accountability demand produces Assembly reform push.

China Chastened

Hardliners discredited. Beijing and Shanghai bombed — punctures "unassailable at home" argument. Moderate faction uses shock to restore control.

India Arrives

Treaty of Delhi establishes India as independent great power. Non-aligned doctrine established deliberately. Long-term counterweight to Chinese regional ambitions.

Russia Pays Price

Backed wrong horse. GPC doubles down on Finnish/Polish independence. Russia begins recalibration.

The Ottoman Fracture

The Ottoman Empire was on the wrong side of the Global War — armed neutral tilting Prussian. The Geneva settlement imposed joint Allied-Ottoman Suez oversight rather than rewarding Ottoman sovereignty. The postwar mood in Istanbul is humiliation, not confidence.

1940s–50s
Postwar consolidation — federal structure established Istanbul establishes genuinely federal arrangement. Syria, Lebanon, Palestine get satisfactory autonomy. Hejaz gets tolerable arrangement. Mesopotamia gets least — oil makes Istanbul intransigent. Egypt pushing for full formalization.
1959–1961
Egypt seizes the Canal Zone — declares independence GPC brokers settlement. Egypt recognized as independent sovereign state. Suez Canal becomes GPC International Zone — administered by GPC, generates revenue funding GPC institutional operations. Settled by 1960/61.
1960s
Relatively quiet — nationalism grows steadily Arab nationalist movements win seats in imperial parliament. Pan-Ottoman project working in the Levant. Mesopotamia increasingly frustrated. Hejaz quietly restless on religious grounds.
1970s
Agitation grows — Mesopotamia the flashpoint Empire's cash cow but most underrepresented. Levant states growing closer to Istanbul (pan-Ottoman working there). Hejaz increasingly at odds with secular modernizer tradition.
Mid-1980s
Breaking point — Hejaz first, then Mesopotamia Hejaz declares independence on religious grounds — relatively clean, Istanbul probably relieved. 1987–89: Mesopotamia erupts in revolt. Kurdish north, Shia south, Sunni center. Ottoman heavy-handedness internationalizes the conflict.
Late 1980s
Holman watches — does not intervene Federalist instinct: cautious about open-ended commitments in messy conflict with no clean good guys. GPC debating but not yet acting.
~1996
Liu joins GPC peacekeeping effort SPA instinct: multilateral, GPC-led, principled. Slow, expensive, increasingly unpopular. Terror attacks on GPC member facilities late 1990s — embassy truck bombs, that scale. Public exhausted.

Rump Ottoman Empire Controls Anatolia, Thrace/Bosphorus, and the Levant (Syria, Lebanon, Palestine — pan-Ottoman project succeeded there). Managing identity crisis of post-imperial existence. Oil role much diminished.

Africa — Postwar Overview

Former Prussian Colonies

Prussian Kongo and Kamerun become GPC trust territories post-Global War. Path to independence in medium-to-long term. Independence in waves through 1950s–1960s. Avoids Allied power scramble for colonial spoils.

Former French West Africa

Decolonizes in a spectrum — not a single moment. Three categories:

  • Early declarers — administratively coherent territories that peeled off during the war, declared for Allies, seek GPC recognition immediately. Senegal most prominent.
  • Negotiated independence — mid-tier territories organized through GPC framework in mid-to-late 1940s.
  • GPC mandate territories — least developed interior regions. Independence timeline stretches into 1950s–1960s.

Sokoto Caliphate — Expansion

Expands into adjacent former French Sahel territories post-war. Ottoman diplomatic backing, legitimate GPC sovereign standing. Absorbs territories with strongest existing commercial and cultural ties. Further expansion contested where it bumps into independent aspirations.

Established Sovereign African States

Ethiopia, Zanzibar, Sokoto — recognized sovereign players with growing GPC diplomatic weight. Push for accelerating independence timelines. Nucleus of pan-African political consciousness.

South Africa

White-governed Commonwealth member. Racial policies becoming systematic. No OTL American Jim Crow as external validation → earlier, stronger international pushback. India's Commonwealth presence creates friction that accelerates India's departure. Racist policies collapse late 1970s under GPC and NAU pressure. Full resolution arc TBD but significant.

Algeria — The Bourbon Monarchy

Framing Note

"Independence" is the wrong frame. The Bourbon monarchy is the Algerian government — there is no colonial power to break from. What develops is a potential republican revolution against a settler monarchy, not a colonial independence movement.

The Dynastic Line

1904–1929
Henri VII — Pretender Bourbon court in Corsica. Keeps French royalist flame alive. Algeria as primary power base.
1929–1939
Henri VIII — Last French pretender Signs extinguishment of French dynastic claim at Geneva 1939 in exchange for Algerian recognition as sovereign state.
1939–~1982
Theodore I — First King of Algeria proper Henri VIII's nephew. Born ~1911, takes throne at 28. Mother from established pied-noir family predating 1904 revolution. Initially puppeted by French nationalist faction. Pushes back mid-1940s. Delivers 1948 constitution. Reigns 40+ years. Beloved elder statesman by end.
~1982 onward
Theodore II — Bridge generation Marries Yasmine (or Nadia — TBD), daughter of prosperous secular Algerian merchant family. Culturally Muslim, not publicly observant. Educated, bilingual. Marriage signals dynasty's genuine Algerian identity. Their children: first generation that is genuinely both.

The 1948 Constitution — Key Provisions

  • Formal citizenship for indigenous Algerians
  • Parliamentary representation for indigenous population
  • Arabic as co-official language alongside French
  • Formal separation of church and state — de-Catholicization. Reverses Corsican court's lean into Catholic identity.

The Settler Population

Higher settler-to-indigenous ratio than OTL — 1904 revolution intensified settlement drive as Algeria became Bourbon power base, not just colony. Three political groups:

  • Pragmatic pied-noir majority — Theodore I's core coalition. Algeria is home; stability serves interests. Accept reform.
  • French nationalist faction — trapped with nowhere to go (UER France is hostile, other colonies becoming republics). Active resistance 1940s–1950s, increasingly marginal by 1960s, absorbed through generational change by 1970s–1980s.
  • Post-1904 and post-1939 arrivals — most resistant. Came specifically under the Bourbon banner.
Long-Term Trajectory

The monarchy survives and becomes genuinely multiethnic — not a colonial state in transition, but a new kind of Mediterranean constitutional monarchy. International community supports reform trajectory; does not push for republican overthrow while genuine progress continues. UER relations permanently awkward but not destabilizing.

Hard Canon

Key US Institutions

USRC — United States Railway Commission

Established 1834. One of America's oldest and most beloved institutions. Permanently vindicated by Global War. Never dismantled — only reformed at margins. Wartime investment produced new rolling stock and urban rail systems.

Department of the Armed Forces

Established post-Global War under Freeman. Oversees: Army, Navy, Air Force, Coast Guard, Home Guard, and National Intelligence. Replaces Department of War.

Home Guard

Centralized version of OTL National Guard. Part militia, part emergency response, part FEMA equivalent. Federal-level commander.

National Intelligence (DNI)

Carved out of Army Intelligence post-Global War. Foreign intelligence gathering only — strict limitation. Equivalent of CIA.

Federal Investigative Service (FIS)

Established C. Roosevelt Administration after Coastal South Uprisings. Under Department of Justice. Domestic investigations only. Strict foreign/domestic intelligence split.

GPC — Global Peace Council

Established Geneva 1939. Headquartered Geneva. Permanent secretariat, regular sessions. Permanent executive council includes NAU, Britain, UER, Russia, China (founding), India (added post-Choson). Suez Canal Zone administered by GPC from ~1961.
