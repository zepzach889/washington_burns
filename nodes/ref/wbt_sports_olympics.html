Institutions & Governance
The World Olympic Committee
Founded 1907

World Olympic Committee (WOC)

Founded by three delegates: Ashcroft (Great Britain), von Altenburg (Prussia), and Bellamonte (Italy). The founding conference convenes in 1907 following informal discussions among European athletic organizations in the preceding years. President Cornelius Roosevelt II sends Peter Sinclair as the American delegate.

First Games: Athens, 1910. Texas and California participate as separate delegations from the outset, distinct from the USA, reflecting their status as sovereign nations.

Structural Note

WOC vs. OTL IOC

The WOC functions analogously to OTL's International Olympic Committee but reflects WBT's different international landscape: Texas and California hold permanent separate delegation status; the North American Union (once formed) does not supersede national Olympic delegations; and the WOC has historically maintained a closer working relationship with the continental America Games model that spread internationally by mid-century.

The United States Olympic Committee (USOC)
Founded 1911

USOC & the America Games

Cornelius Roosevelt II becomes the first USOC head in 1911 and immediately establishes the America Games — a centralized domestic trials competition whose winners feed the US Olympic team. Scoped initially to Olympic core disciplines: track and field, swimming, gymnastics, rowing, tennis, boxing, weightlifting, fencing, shooting, cycling. Baseball, stickball, Aroball, and rugby sit entirely outside this system. Football is added to the Olympic program at a later date (TBD), with the WFA coordinating the Olympic tournament — national teams competing against national teams, functioning as this timeline's equivalent of OTL's World Cup.

The America Games model proves influential. By mid-century, analogous domestic trials programs exist across the major Olympic nations — some named for the American model, others independently developed.

Calendar Structure

Summer & Winter Cycles

From 1913, Summer America Games are held the year before each Summer Olympics, giving the selected team a full season to prepare. The WOC announces a Winter Olympics cycle at some point before 1922; the first Winter America Games are held in 1921, selecting the US team for the inaugural 1922 Hugosville Winter Games.

From 1984 onward, Winter and Summer Games operate on a two-year offset cycle, with dedicated Summer and Winter America Games permanently staggered. The first separate-year Winter Games (1984 Crossings, Washingtonia) was hosted by the USA.

Key Structural Rules
Delegation Structure

Separate North American Nations

The USA, Canada, Texas (Federation of Texas), and California (Union of the Californias) each field their own Olympic delegations. Hawaii, Cuba, Puerto Rico, and other NAU members similarly maintain independent delegations. The NAU does not produce a combined team.

Big Sports

Olympic & Non-Olympic

Baseball, stickball, Aroball, and rugby are not Olympic sports in WBT. Football (soccer) is added to the Olympic program at a date TBD — the WFA coordinates with the WOC, and the Olympic football tournament functions as the premier national-team competition (equivalent to OTL's World Cup). The WFA's own Global Cup is a franchise/club competition, not a national-team event.

Cancelled Games

Global War Interruption

The 1934 Toronto Summer and Winter Games were cancelled when Canada entered the Global War. The 1938 Madrid Summer and Winter Games were similarly cancelled. An eight-year gap (1930–1942) separates the last prewar Games from the first postwar celebration in San Francisco (hosted by the Union of the Californias).

The America Games Calendar

The America Games are the United States' domestic Olympic trials competition, held the year before each Summer and Winter Games. Established in 1911 by Cornelius Roosevelt II as USOC head; first edition held 1913 (feeding 1914 London). Scoped to Olympic core disciplines only — the Big Sports operate outside this system entirely.

From 1984, the Summer and Winter editions of the America Games operate on permanently staggered two-year cycles, mirroring the new Olympic calendar offset. Prior to 1984, Summer and Winter America Games were held in the same year.

Section Five
Nations & Delegations
North American Delegations
Full Member — Since 1910

United States of America

Full WOC member since founding. Governed by the USOC (est. 1911). Has hosted Summer Games three times: 1922 Boston, 1986 Manhattan, 2026 Philadelphia. Has hosted Winter Games three times: 1922 Hugosville, 1984 Crossings, 2024 Lewis.

Full Member — Since 1910

Canada

Full WOC member since founding. Hosted Summer and Winter both in 1950 (Toronto/Vancouver). Was scheduled to host 1934 (cancelled, Global War). Consistently strong Olympic program across Summer and Winter disciplines.

Separate Delegation — Since 1910

Federation of Texas

Texas fields its own delegation, fully separate from the USA, reflecting its status as a sovereign nation. Has hosted Summer Games once: 1958 Houston (city name possibly different ITTL). Strong in football (soccer), Aroball, and track and field.

Separate Delegation — Since 1910

Union of the Californias

California fields its own delegation, fully separate from the USA. Commonwealth immigration (Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, Fiji) gives California distinct Pacific-facing athletic strengths. Competes as its own delegation in rugby, giving it international rugby rivalries distinct from the rest of North America.

Associate / Emerging — TBD

Hawaii, Mexico, Cuba, Puerto Rico

NAU members that maintain independent Olympic delegations. Hawaii potentially participates as a North American associate member from the 1942 San Francisco Games (hosted by California). Specific delegation histories to be developed.

Notable International Situations
1926–1954

UER Absence & Return

The UER withdraws from the Olympics under Holtz following the 1926 Hamburg Games — notable partly because Hamburg itself is in Prussia, which the UER controls, making the context pointed. The UER returns by 1954 Paris, hosting both that year's Summer and Winter Games. The absence is shorter than it might have seemed at the time.

1992

Choson at Wonsan

A unified, internationally engaged Choson hosting the 1992 Winter Olympics 25 years after the Choson War (1966–68) is itself a quiet statement of postwar success. The contrast with the wartime shadow over the 1966 Stockholm Summer Games is deliberate.
