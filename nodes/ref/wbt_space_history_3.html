Foundation conditions The Global War (1932–1940) accelerates rocketry and aerospace research across all major belligerents. NAU rocketry R&D runs through the Continental Defense Command, formally established spring 1942. The NAU fission program is tested in 1951; China tests fission in early 1952. Mature rocket engineering plus a nuclear-armed rival create the structural conditions for a sustained space program. NASA's model is hybrid from the start: genuine scientific infrastructure, with competitive prestige as a secondary driver that intensifies when China achieves specific milestones.
Phase I
Foundation
1947–1958 — Priest and pre-Priest era
1947–48 North American Space Agency founded Freeman / Nolan

Spun out of CDC rocketry research as its own joint NAU institution. USA, Canada, California, and Texas are primary partners; Mexico participates with observer/limited status reflecting its NAU opt-out posture. Early mandate covers high-altitude atmospheric research, meteorological observation, and communications relay — framed publicly as scientific and peaceful. Military rocketry connection is obvious and not denied; NASA and CDC maintain close institutional relationship throughout this era.

Open CanvasExact founding date and enabling legislation name. NASA headquarters location — likely a US city, possibly Franklin or a Sun Belt location with existing aerospace infrastructure. Whether Britain's Commonwealth space program exists at this stage.
early 1950s Pioneer satellite program authorized Nolan

Series of progressively more capable research satellites authorized. Intelligence community tracking Chinese rocketry program with growing concern. The Pioneer program is primarily a scientific instrument, but its acceleration in mid-decade will be driven by geopolitical urgency.

1957 Pioneer One — world's first artificial satellite Priest (JCA)

Intelligence indicates China is 18–24 months from their own satellite capability; the Priest administration accelerates the Pioneer timeline to ensure the NAU launches first. The launch is a major international moment, broadcast continent-wide. Priest receives the credit — it is one of the few achievements of his administration that history treats without ambivalence. Pioneer One is primarily a research and telemetry satellite; practical applications follow in Pioneer Two and Three (weather, early communications).

Phase II
The Moon Program
1959–1977 — Lincoln through Langston
1959–60 Moon program announced Lincoln (SPA)

Lincoln's early presidency includes a major NASA address framing the moon as humanity's next horizon — explicitly multilateral in framing, not nationalist. Congress approves expanded funding; the lunar program gets a name, budget, and a target timeline aimed at the late 1960s. Lincoln frames the program ideologically: the same cooperative, pluralist model that won the Global War will put humans on the moon.

Open CanvasName of the lunar program (analog to OTL Apollo but distinct). Whether Lincoln's address has a specific memorable formulation.
1962 China's first satellite Lincoln (SPA)

Triggers the "competitive jolt" that converts NASA from a scientific infrastructure project into a prestige race in public consciousness. NAU funding surges; congressional debate shifts from "should we?" to "how fast?" China simultaneously announces a crewed spaceflight program, closing the perceived gap faster than expected.

1963–65 First NAU crewed orbital missions Lincoln (SPA)

Late Lincoln years see the first humans in orbit under the NASA program. Multi-national crews from the start: American mission commanders standard, Canadian and Californian crew members in early missions. Public enthusiasm at a high; the moon feels achievable within the decade.

Disruption
Major
Choson War, 1966–1968/69 (Schultz administration) — Direct NAU troop involvement; genuine military commitment, not burden-sharing. Aerospace engineers pulled toward military satellite and secure communications work. Moon program timeline slips from "late 1960s" to "early 1970s" — publicly acknowledged, politically controversial. Schultz (Federalist) commits fully despite the awkward optics of a Federalist fighting the same adversary her predecessors were accused of appeasing. NASA budget cut but program not cancelled; institutional momentum too strong.
1966–69 Choson War — program disrupted Schultz (Fed)

Moon program slips approximately five years. Choson War ends with NAU withdrawal and Choson's independence secured; China does not achieve its objectives. Langston wins the 1970 election partly on program restoration and postwar renewal. NASA reinvestment is a first-budget priority under Langston, with bipartisan support given the program's popularity.

Open CanvasWhether Schultz attempts to protect NASA funding explicitly or accepts cuts as war prioritization. Exact Choson War peace terms and their long-term implications.
1975 First crewed lunar orbit Langston (SPA)

Three-person crew: one American commander, one Canadian, one Californian. Major international broadcast; genuine NAU-wide celebration. China observes; their own crewed lunar program is approximately eight to ten years behind at this point. The orbit mission is treated as a near-milestone — the landing is clearly imminent.

1977 First moon landing Langston (SPA)

Soft Canon — year confirmed Crew composition: American, Canadian, and Californian at minimum. The landing is framed as a NAU achievement and a human achievement — deliberately internationalist in public messaging. Langston explicitly connects it to Lincoln's vision; the JCA argues that Schultz's war cost a decade of delay; Federalists cite the CDC and Pioneer program as the true foundation.

Phase III
Flags, Footprints, and First Steps Toward Permanence
1977–1989 — Whitmore through early Pike
1979–83 Flags and footprints phase Whitmore (Dem)

Several additional landing missions, increasingly scientific in character. A Whitmore-era mission leaves a small automated research station on the surface — the first permanent human-made structure on the moon. Public enthusiasm remains high but the program normalizes; it becomes infrastructure rather than historic drama. Serious discussion begins about permanent crewed presence, but no decision is made.

Open CanvasNumber and locations of Whitmore-era missions.
~1982–84 China lands on the moon Whitmore / Pike

The competitive jolt that converts the permanent base question from scientific to political. China's landing is a genuine achievement; NAU reaction is measured publicly and urgent privately. Pike (takes office 1983) does not frame it as panic — he frames it as inevitability: "The question was never if humans would live on the moon. The question was who would be first to call it home."

Open CanvasExact year of Chinese landing within this range.
1984–85 Lunar Settlement Act Pike (SPA)

Congress authorizes permanent crewed lunar base. Pike connects the decision explicitly to his broader themes: the expansion of who belongs, and the expansion of where humanity can go. Base site is selected; construction is targeted for completion by the late 1980s. The Act is one of the defining legislative achievements of Pike's presidency alongside marriage equality.

Open CanvasName and location of the base — Pike administration would favor something forward-looking and multilateral, not commemorative. Base site selection likely near a pole for water ice access.
Phase IV
Permanent Presence and the Ottoman Disruption
1989–2007 — Holman through Mercer
1989–92 Base construction and first permanent crew Pike / Holman (Fed)

Construction begins under Pike; first permanent rotating crew arrives under Holman. Initial crew of four to six people, rotating annually, primarily scientific mandate. Holman maintains Pike's framing and commitment — it is a genuinely bipartisan achievement; neither party wants to be seen opposing it.

Open CanvasComposition of first permanent rotation. Nationalities represented beyond USA and Canada.
1992–98 Base growth and early economic rationale Holman / Liu (SPA)

Crew size grows to ten to fifteen; mission duration extended. Economic rationale begins developing: helium-3 extraction research, materials science, early commercial research partnerships. Liu initiates a robotic Mars probe program in earnest. Britain's Commonwealth space program (UK/Australia/India) achieves crewed orbital capability.

Disruption
Moderate
Ottoman Fracture / GPC involvement in Mesopotamia, late 1990s–early 2000s — GPC burden-sharing operation; NAU is a significant contributor but not the lead. Disruption is shallower than the Choson War: no direct large-scale ground war for the NAU. NASA budget plateaus rather than cuts; the crewed Mars program is pushed back; lunar base growth slows. Robotic Mars program continues relatively unaffected — probes are cheap relative to troops.
2001–07 Plateau continues Mercer (Dem)

Mercer prioritizes GPC commitments and postwar stabilization; space is not a signature issue. Lunar base reaches twenty to twenty-five residents; growth continues but unspectacularly. European joint space program (UER / German Republic / Italy / Spain) achieves crewed orbital capability during this period.

Open CanvasExact year European program achieves crewed orbital flight. Whether Mercer makes any space policy moves of note.
Phase V
Renewal and Internationalization
2007–2019 — Sinclair through Hudson
2007–13 Base comes of age — European integration Sinclair (Dem)

Ottoman Fracture winding down; resources freeing up. Lunar base reaches thirty to forty residents and begins to feel like a genuine community rather than a research outpost. First non-NAU permanent crew members: European program astronauts join the base under a formal framework agreement. Base develops nascent permanent infrastructure: hydroponic food production, expanded medical facilities, early commercial research partnerships.

Open CanvasName and structure of the NAU-European space cooperation framework. Whether any early space tourism milestones occur in this period.
2013–19 Mars program reinvestment Hudson (Fed)

Hudson treats Mars as a bipartisan infrastructure legacy project — deliberately not framed as ideological. Major crewed Mars program funding authorization; target is a crewed Mars orbital mission in the late 2020s. Lunar base reaches fifty or more residents; first signs of genuine permanent community culture developing. China's lunar presence expands significantly during this period.

Phase VI
The Race to Mars
2019–2031 — Clearwater and Nakamura
2019–25 Final preparation — two programs on the pad Clearwater

Robotic surface program delivering site surveys and resource assessments. Both the NAU and Chinese programs are visibly in final preparation simultaneously; intelligence about rival launch readiness, public speculation about timelines, and political pressure not to rush at the cost of crew safety define the atmosphere. Lunar base at sixty to eighty residents; first serious discussions of permanent non-rotating residents. European program fully integrated into both lunar operations and Mars planning.

By this period the NAU-China relationship has matured into a friendly rivalry rather than a tense standoff — forty-plus years since the Choson War, decades of parallel lunar presence, and shared GPC obligations have normalized the relationship considerably. The race is real but not existential.

Open CanvasClearwater's party affiliation (Socialist or Federalist). Whether any Mars-adjacent milestones — final robotic pathfinders, hardware tests — fall in Clearwater's term and become public markers of the race.
late 2026 NAU Mars mission launches Nakamura (SPA)

Mission launches in the late 2026 window. Nakamura inherits a program that is essentially on the pad when she takes office in January 2025 — the heavy infrastructure work was done under Hudson and Clearwater. The crew is highly international: NAU members at minimum, plus European program and likely British Commonwealth representation; possibly a neutral nation such as Russia or Japan.

Note: there is no orbital-only precursor mission. The travel time between Earth and Mars — six to nine months each way — makes an orbit-and-return mission an unacceptable waste of a launch window. The first crewed Mars mission lands, full stop.

2027 First humans land on Mars — NAU mission Nakamura (SPA)

Soft Canon — year confirmed The NAU mission lands on Mars in 2027, one of the defining moments of Nakamura's presidency and of the 21st century. The parallel to 1977 is explicit in public framing — the same way Langston connected the moon landing to Lincoln's vision, Nakamura connects Mars to the full arc from Pioneer One through Pike's Lunar Settlement Act. The crew is the most internationally diverse in spaceflight history.

Open CanvasLanding site. Exact date. The specific formulation of whoever steps out first. Whether the moment is shaped by any awareness of where China's mission stands.
2029–30 China lands on Mars Nakamura (SPA)

Soft Canon — range confirmed China's mission arrives two to three years after the NAU landing — close enough that historians will argue about what might have been, far enough that there is no ambiguity about who won. The NAU crew may still be on the surface or in transit home when China arrives; the two missions are likely at different sites but in radio contact.

The moment is warm rather than frosty. By 2029 the friendly-rivalry framing has taken hold; the NAU publicly congratulates the Chinese crew, a joint broadcast is likely. The image of two flags on Mars — two great powers, both standing on the same red desert, talking to each other across a few hundred kilometers — becomes one of the iconic images of the era. China's landing is a genuine national achievement and is celebrated as such, even in the NAU.
