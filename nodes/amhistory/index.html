National Archives Gallery
Permanent Installation · Lower Level · Free Timed Entry
The Founding Documents of the United States
The Museum of the United States is the permanent home of the nation's three founding documents, held in climate-controlled preservation vaults and displayed in the National Archives Gallery. Visitors may view the original parchments under secure, controlled conditions.
Declaration of Independence · 1776 Constitution of the United States · 1787 Constitution of the United States · 1861
Timed entry passes required. Photography permitted without flash. Documents may occasionally be removed from display for conservation.

Featured Exhibition
Special Exhibition — Now Open
The Burning and the Building: Washington, August 1814
A landmark exhibition examining the destruction of the federal city on the Potomac and the extraordinary choices that followed — the debates, the journeys, and the founding of Franklin on the Ohio. Drawing on objects, maps, and personal accounts from the National Collection, the exhibition traces how a catastrophe became a founding moment.

Permanent Galleries
Level 1 · East Wing
The American Presidency: From Washington to Nakamura
Two centuries of executive leadership — objects, documents, and portraits tracing every president from the republic's founding to the present day.
Level 1 · West Wing
Iron Nation: Two Centuries of American Rail
From the founding of the USRC in 1834 to the modern high-speed network — the story of how rail shaped the American continent.
Level 2 · Central Hall
War Between the States, 1857–1860: The Crisis That Remade America
The defining conflict of the nineteenth century — from the collapse of the Hawthorne government to the Philadelphia Convention and the Constitution of 1861.
Level 2 · North Gallery
The Global War: America and the World, 1932–1940
America's defining international conflict — from the first Prussian advances to the Treaty of Geneva and the founding of the Global Peace Council.
Level 3 · South Gallery
Votes and Voices: Two Centuries of American Democracy
From the first elections under the 1787 Constitution to the Third Amendment and universal suffrage — the long arc of democratic participation in America.
Level 3 · East Gallery
American Arts & Material Culture, 1814–Present
Objects, artworks, and everyday items tracing American material life from the founding of Franklin to the present — furniture, fashion, fine art, and folk tradition.
