The National Archives Gallery is the permanent home of the three founding documents of the United States, held in climate-controlled preservation vaults on the Lower Level of the Museum. The original parchments are displayed under secure, controlled conditions. Select a document below to read the full annotated text.

Visitor Information Timed entry passes are required for the National Archives Gallery. Passes may be reserved in advance or collected from the Lower Level information desk. The gallery is open Tuesday through Sunday, 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Photography is permitted without flash. Documents may occasionally be removed from display for conservation work.

Declaration of Independence
Adopted 4 July 1776
The founding statement of the American republic, asserting the right of the people to self-governance and establishing the philosophical basis of the new nation.

Constitution of the United States
Ratified 21 June 1788
The original constitutional framework of the United States, establishing the three branches of government and the foundational law of the republic. Superseded by the Constitution of 1861.

Constitution of the United States
Adopted 15 May 1861
The revised constitutional order adopted by the Philadelphia Convention, abolishing slavery, establishing universal citizenship, and restructuring the executive branch. Currently in force.
