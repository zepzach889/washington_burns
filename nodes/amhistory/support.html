Admission to the Museum has been free since opening day in 1879. That promise is kept by an annual appropriation from Congress — and by the generosity of members, donors, and volunteers who believe the history of the republic belongs to everyone. Every membership, gift, and volunteered hour keeps the doors open to all.

Giving in the Founders' Tradition
Named for the year President Littlefield opened the Museum's doors, the 1879 Society brings together the Museum's most devoted patrons. Society gifts have underwritten gallery renovations, major acquisitions for the National Collection, and the conservation of the founding documents themselves. Members gather each February on the anniversary of opening day for the Founders' Dinner in the Great Hall of the Republic.

Volunteer

More than two hundred volunteers serve the Museum — as gallery docents leading the daily highlights tours, as greeters in the Great Hall of the Republic, and as assistants supporting Family Days and school visits. Volunteers receive full training from the Museum's education staff, and no prior experience is required beyond a love of the republic's history. Prospective volunteers may enquire at the information desk.

Gifts & Bequests

Gifts to the Museum's endowment secure its work for generations to come, and bequests have enriched the National Collection since the earliest days — many of the presidential items on display entered the Collection as gifts from the families of the presidents themselves. The Museum also welcomes gifts of objects and documents of historical significance, which are reviewed by the curatorial departments.

Corporations and foundations support the Museum through exhibition sponsorship and program underwriting; the semiquincentennial exhibition The Republic at 250 is supported in part by such gifts.

How to Give Memberships may be purchased at the information desk or by post. Enquiries regarding major gifts, bequests, and corporate support are handled by the Museum's development office, Union Avenue, Franklin, D.W.
