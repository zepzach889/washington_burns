The National Collection is the material record of the American republic — more than one million objects, artworks, and documents held in the public trust. Only a fraction is on view in the galleries at any time; the Collection as a whole is a working national resource, drawn upon for exhibitions, research, conservation, and loans to institutions across the Union.

John Trumbull & the Art of the Republic
The Museum holds the nation's foremost collection of works by John Trumbull. The three great canvases displayed in the Great Hall of the Republic are joined in the Collection by preparatory studies, sketches, and correspondence documenting how the defining images of the republic came to be painted.

Conservation & the Preservation Vaults

Beneath the public galleries, the Museum's Lower Level houses the preservation vaults — the climate-controlled facilities that safeguard the founding documents and the most fragile holdings of the National Collection. The Museum's conservation staff care for everything from eighteenth-century parchment to wartime textiles and photographic material, and objects throughout the galleries are periodically withdrawn from display for treatment and rest.

The vaults have held the original parchments of the Declaration of Independence and both Constitutions since their transfer to the Museum in 1902. When not undergoing conservation, the documents are displayed in the National Archives Gallery.

Loans & Access

The National Collection belongs to the people of the United States, and the Museum works to make it available far beyond Franklin. Objects from the Collection are on loan to museums, historical societies, and university galleries across the Union at any given time, and the Museum's curators regularly assist institutions preparing exhibitions of their own.

Scholars, students, and members of the public may request access to objects and documents not currently on display through the Museum's research services, operated in partnership with the University of the United States.

Researching the Collection Study-room appointments, image requests, and research inquiries are handled through the Museum's Learn & Research services. Requests for items held in the preservation vaults require advance arrangement.
