What if Washington Never Rose Again?
On August 24, 1814, British forces burned Washington City. In our history, it was rebuilt. In this timeline, it was not — and the two centuries that followed diverged profoundly from our own.

This is the web home of Washington Burns, an alternate history timeline exploring what might have happened if the British destruction of Washington City had been more thorough and more permanent.

In this world, the United States abandoned its original capital and relocated westward, founding Franklin in the District of Washington on the Ohio River — where Cincinnati stands in our own timeline.

The aftermath of the War of 1812 proved far worse for the young republic. Territory was ceded to Britain. A threatened revolt from New England forced sweeping constitutional reforms. The presidency became a single six-year term. The Federalist Party, which vanished by the 1820s in our history, survived here to become one of four enduring major parties.

A slightly earlier Civil War — Northern secession, not Southern — reshaped the nation from 1857 to 1860. A different analog to the Great War left France as the heart of a communist federation. The 20th century unfolded on its own strange terms, toward a present that is both familiar and deeply different.

Originally written 2017–2020, the timeline is currently undergoing a full rewrite. The rewritten chapters are what you'll find here. Join the discussion at AlternateHistory.com.
